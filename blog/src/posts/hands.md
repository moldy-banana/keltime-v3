When I was little, I found a bottle of pink nail polish stuffed into the red stockings over the fireplace that we never used on Christmas day. It was the cheap, watery kind that was marketed as "safe for kids". I made a habit of painting my nails and then peeling off the polish like a banana, though it never came out quite right because my paint jobs were terrible. 

Chewing and cutting my nails became a habit through swim and piano. Every time I got out of the freezing cold pool, put on my TYR branded parka, and slung my SPEEDO swim bag (that everybody carried) over my shoulder, I took a good look at my nailsand chewed them short. Over COVID I quit swimming and piano and shut myself into my room. I didn't notice how long my nails got until they started cutting the rubber padding off of my mouse. I remember admiring my nails and thinking: "Wow, my nails are finally long now!" and I stared at the crescent shaped parts until I promptly chewed them off again. 


Growing pains came with being increasingly self conscious and insecure. I started to observe people's hands and nails. Straight. Clean. Manicured. Symmetrical. Thin. And then I started comparing them to my own (as all insecurities progress). Crooked. Nasty. Lopsided. Bumpy. Thick. I still chewed at my nails. I remember when my neighbor introduced me to pushing back the nail cuticles. She pushed the cuticles of her long, straight nails back before applying cuticle oil. 


Then I became self conscious of my tastes. My "pinterest boards". I discovered the "do these 5 exercises and massages every day for straighter, longer fingers!" I saved them immediately on my "glow up" board. I followed them for a max of 4 days before forgetting about them entirely. Entering high school, other worries and responsibilities stacked up. Hands and nails simply weren't a priority anymore. I would like to think that insecurities were the least of my problems. 

This year, I've become fond of using a nail file to trim down my nails. They are very fun to use. I like how smooth they make the edges of my nails. It's theraputic, the way that the nail slowly disappears. Whenever I'm stressed or upset, I end up filing my nails somehow. This happens almost every day... 


"Okay Desi why are you telling me this??" You may ask. This is information about me, for me, that only I know about. Nobody knows this except for me and the people reading this blogpost now, I guess. It's important to remember these things about yourself. One day, I'm going to forget that I used to chew my nails while walking across the pool deck smelling like chlorine. I'm going to forget that I filed and rounded my nails on a daily basis, hoping they would appear more uniformly. That would be pretty sad. Oh hey, the USPS trucks changed to the newest model. 

tldr: nothing stays the same.
