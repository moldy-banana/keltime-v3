Malcom Todd, Audrey Hobert, and Eve. I yap the most about Eve.

I made time to listen to Malcolm Todd's "new" album, Do It Again. By that I mean I procrastinated my obligations and homework. My teammates have been singing the chorus to Chest Pain (I Love) for a while, yet I had never listened to it.

Therefore, I resolved to listen to it after listening to Do It Again and I luckily found the music video. Guys. How does it only have 2.4 million views?? ts underrated. The visualizer for the same song has nearly 15 times the views, which is unfortunate because the music video is quite well made and entertaining. "I DM'ed you 365 times. I DM'ed you every day for OVER A YEAR!" Listen to it here.

The music video was so entertaining in fact that I then sought to consume more of his music videos (and visualizers). The one for I Just Got Mad was pretty fun. Yet the one that stood out the most to me was Roommates. I get amused easily, but the juxtapositions were delightful to watch.

Where Malcolm Todd is "nonchalant" in his music videos, Audrey is eccentric and charming. Sue Me, Bowling Alley, Thirst Trap, and Phoebe all have pretty intense dancing, though Phoebe is shot in a style more like Malcom Todd's videos. I think that Audrey (though some say a 'nepo baby' like Malcolm) is underrated. She has a geniune, fun vibe around her and her music is good. Just from the album cover of Who's The Clown?, you can derive some of her personality:

I like it. I really love her vibe. It's something you should experience for yourself.

I've been looking forward to Eve's new music video for a long time now. It's part of a hexalogy of sorts featuring three main characters + a dragon girl drawn and animated by Mariyasu. Each character gets two videos- the first one is always the intro/exposition while the second is more like a conclusion. Tobi got How to Eat Life and Fight Song and Fuchi (my favorite) got INSOMNIA (20 million views!!) and Sayonara End Roll. Mahiru's first music video and introduction was Ghost Avenue. The latest one, called AIYOU has a super distinct and attractive visual style like many "vintage" shot anime EDs (ex. JJK ED: More Than Words by Hitsujibungaku).

At first I couldn't even tell whose point of view the video was being sung from. It was hard to find a "main character" as all of the characters were featured frequently. Spoilers ahead!! Please watch AIYOU here and turn on closed captions!!; I honestly think this song sounds amazing.

As Eve sings, "So from here, I wave goodbye," Mahiru waves directly at the screen, and I realise that he's probably never coming back. He says directly: "From here on our paths will never cross / You're the only one that knows that". But who is Mahiru saying that to? Who is the one that knows?

Is it Mahiru's friend Tobi, or is it his half brother Fuchi? Or maybe even Ryuko? The quick answer is that it's Fuchi. Fuchi is the one he's talking to- more onto that later.

If there's one thing I can't do, it is figuring out the meaning of lyrics. Mahiru states many mystifying things in AIYOU (as translated by the closed captions- and they're also in COURIER NEW?? my favorite!!). Such as:

  • "I want to be with a you that isn't you"
  • "This night doesn't only belong to you"
  • "I'm going mad with jealousy / And somehow I'm the only one who's lost my mind, or so it seems"
  • "I want to see a me that isn't me / All these answers I can never derive / If only I could decipher this astronomical equation"
  • "It [my heart] wants to forget everything / In this future where the words I want to say never reach you"

In the lyrics as a whole, I read it as Mahiru learning to care and love his half brother Fuchi (and probably the friend group as a whole). But Mahiru also expresses wishes of "letting go of this world" and going to a place where "No one will ever find me".

You could argue "Bro this is just the ED for Apothecary Diaries season 3 it's not that serious bro the lyrics are probably about Jinshi and is yearning for Maomao so these lyrics don't apply to Mahiru."

But there must be a reason why this music video and AIYOU were paired together. The pattern has been two songs per character. Tobi got two, Fuchi got two, and the addition of this one would give Mahiru two as well. It must be that this is Mahiru's "conclusion" video, where he learns how to love the people around them (after Ghost Avenue, which was quite a bleak song).

This is quite long already, so I want to conclude with my final thoughts about the song itself. Eve has been embracing a pop style for a while now, straying away from guitar heavy hooks and intros like in his prior albums Bunka, Otogi, and Smile. He's also been singing in a softer voice (like in AIYOU). I distinctly remember him having a fuller voice in songs 5+ years ago, not using a softer tone as much.

Eve's music quality and singing has definitely improved in range. AIYOU is full of emotion and the song moves my heart in a nostalgic way (like more than words by Hitsujibungaku). I hate to say that it's better than Fuchi's songs, but I prefer AIYOU to Sayonara End Roll. I'm so happy Eve fed us a banger MV with even more banger music.

Today I said something super embarrassing that I shouldn't have said. I didn't even realise the implications of it till I said it. I told one of the best high school horn players in my district that I was going to accept being a mediocre horn player. I told him that I believed that at some point, some of the talent had to be innate.

THAT. WAS. SO. FUCKING. STUPID!!! Not only did I totally ignore the probably countless hours that the actually good people spend practicing, IT WAS SO BAD AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHh I'm currently reevaluating my position on life and I want to go hide underground for the rest of my life.

The truth is that I have been knowingly and unknowingly digging myself into a hole for years. I had slowly started to believe that in the end I wouldn't ever add up to anything because I wasn't born "smart" or "quick-minded" like all the other prodigies I saw around me.

I had truly started to believe in the ridiculous notion that talent was fixed. That I couldn't get any better at playing the horn. That I would never, ever, ever for the rest of my life, amount to anything at all. And I told myself repeatedly the same mantra. And then I said it in front of someone else, for the first time.

It's been a long time coming. This was a path that I should have noticed -that I probably did notice- but still let myself go down. Self destruction, am I right? So I wrote myself a little sticky note. The things below are important lines that I hope to never forget for the rest of my life:

  • Intelligence and talent can grow.
  • I can learn anything I want.
  • My EFFORT and ATTITUDE determine my abilities.
  • Skills are BUILT, not born.
  • I can improve with effort and practice.

It feels kind of corny. I feel like a cornball. Whatever, I guess it's time to go practice the horn now...
